## Why every earlier Kanban was wrong
Jo's board (live and phase 2) assigned its three post-approval lanes from `hash(id) % 3`, derived the number of approval steps from the amount and "steps done" from a status string, and let "next" orders be approved out of turn. Ours had real states (Pending / Approved / Rejected) but no notion of whose turn it was: approvers were a display list, anyone could drag anything, and Rejected was a lane. Both put the approver's turn on the status axis. In the system it lives on the approval path.

## The legacy model (MBAccounting, `POOrderRepository.cs`; see the reference memory for file pointers)
- One record status: Unapproved (0), Approved (1), Closed (2), Voided (3). **Rejected is never stored**: it is a rejected approval row on an Unapproved request. **Paid is never stored**: it is derived from the linked AP invoice's posted journal.
- Two paths per record: the Approval Path (request) and the Payment Approval Path (per invoice, after approval). A path is ordered levels: "Then" opens a new level, "Or" adds an interchangeable approver to the same level; each approver has a dollar threshold below which the level is skipped. Any one approver satisfies a level; approving at a higher level implies the lower ones (the server does not enforce order; the grid and the "awaiting next" query do).
- "Awaiting my approval next" = I am on the path and `Max(acted Sequence) + 1` is my level. "Awaiting my approval" = I am on the path and have not acted, wherever my level sits. Hold is a per-row reason (and a known legacy bug: it counts as acted, so the next level sees the request as theirs).

## What the rebuild does (ours)
- **Lanes are derived**, never the raw status: Pending approval (Unapproved) | Payment approval (Approved, payment path open) | Ready to pay (payment approved, no check) | Paid (check posted); Closed / Voided stay in the table and the Finish column. Selecting a lane on the status chip shows that lane alone.
- **Turn badge on every card**, from the live path: "Your approval next (level k of n)", "Your approval later. Waiting on X", "Waiting on A or B (level k of n, payment)", "Rejected by X: reason", "On hold: reason". Only a card the viewer can act on (their turn, Ready to pay, Paid) is draggable.
- **Scope chip** with the legacy vocabulary: Awaiting my approval next / Awaiting my approval / All requests. Headline = requests awaiting me; context line = pending count and outstanding; Glance = the same figure with Pending / Payment / Ready tiles.
- **A drop is an act on the live path**: Pending approval -> Payment approval records my approval (completing the request path when every applicable level is satisfied, which sets Approved); Payment approval -> Ready to pay records my payment approval; Ready to pay -> Paid enters the payment; Paid -> Close; unpaid -> Void. One lane at a time; refusals name the turn.
- **Approvals tab**: one row per level of the path ("Starts with / Then / Ends with A or B (from $N)"), Approved / Rejected / Hold boxes enabled on the viewer's level only, "Implied by a higher level" and "Skipped: below this level's threshold" states, updated-by per row; a second grid for the Payment Approval Path once Approved. A turn line at the top states whose approval is next.
- **Demo data** carries acted rows on both paths (`appr`, `payAppr`) with threshold and Or cases, rejected and held rows, and payment-approved and paid records.

## 27 Sep: thresholds are per approver, paths are whatever the organisation builds
The owner's real Education Ministry path (Start with Alfred Johnson from $500, Then Jim AndersonAndMoreLetters or Lanette Stewart from $2,000, Ends with Pastor Bob from $5,000) exposed two gaps, both fixed (`_tools/one-off/build-w13-per-user-thresholds-2026-09-27.js`):
- **Minimums belong to the approver, not the level** (PO_ApprovalPath.RequiredMinimum is per user row). A level applies when at least one of its approvers' minimum is reached, and only those approvers can act on it; an Or level with different minimums narrows to the approvers the total reaches. The Approvals tab prints each approver's own minimum and marks a level "Skipped: below every minimum on this level".
- **A request no level applies to** (every minimum above the total, e.g. $430 on that path) is released on approval without an approver, as the legacy ApproveOrder does on submit; the card reads "No approver required at $430.00 on Education Ministry. Approve to release."
Demo: PO-2899 ($640) waits on Alfred alone; PO-2891 ($3,150) has Alfred approved and Lanette holding at the Or level with Pastor Bob unreached; PO-2907 ($430) is the release case; PO-2633 ($15) was released on both paths.

**Why this was right.** `PO_Company.UsePaymentApprovalProcess` is a real company flag whose DB default is **0, off** (`Plugin/sql/Archive/1045_V810_SchemaUpdate.sql:24`; entity `Shelby.Data/EntityClasses/POCompany.cs:512`; the checkbox at `PurchasingManagement/CompanyInformation/Default.aspx.cs:46,86`). With it off the payment path does not exist: the Payment Approval tab and the payment-path dropdown are hidden (`Requests/Update.aspx:93`), `PO_Order.PaymentApprovalID` stays null so the whole invoice-approval hydration block is skipped (`POOrderRepository.cs:322`, `:1489`), no `PO_OrderInvoice` rows are created, `ApprovedForPayment` is never set, `CreateInvoiceInAccountsPayable` is forced off (`CompanyInformation/Default.aspx.cs:87`), and the Approval Paths admin collapses to `ForRequest` paths only (`ApprovalPaths/Default.aspx.cs:25-51`). An approved PO goes straight to AP to be entered and paid by hand. Decisive: **the legacy's own Purchasing Management data panel never reads payment at all** (`DataPanelControls/PurchasingManagement.ascx.cs:80-122`) - it models the request path only, with exactly our four statuses. Three of our four lanes were payment-derived, so on such a company the board was three-quarters empty and showed a workflow the customer does not have.

### The rulings
1. **A code constant, no UI.** `PURF_USE_PAY`, default `false`. Both boards stay in the file: flipping the constant restores the payment board unchanged. That is also what keeps the ~150 lane and payment assertions alive rather than deleting them.
2. **The Kanban forces one path.** The path chip drops "All approval paths" on the board, lists every path (not only the ones with rows, so a quiet path still shows its levels) and defaults to the first, Administration. The columns can then name real approvers.
3. **Column headers are approver names**, with the threshold shown - owner: "Names only but think of Ideas of showing the threshold limits". Three ways, below.
4. **The setting reaches the whole widget, following legacy**, not the Kanban alone.

### What the path board is
One column per level of the selected path, in Sequence order, then **Approved**, then the existing Close / Void Finish column. A card sits in the column of the level that has to act next. Closed and Voided stay off the board, as before; rejected and held cards keep their badge and stay in the column they are parked at. A request no level applies to (PO-2907 at $430) sits in the first column with its "Approve to release" badge, as legacy `ApproveOrder` releases it on submit (`POOrderRepository.cs:1766-1768`).

**Thresholds show three ways.** A muted sub-line in the header ("from $500"), rendered only when the level's minimum is above zero, so Administration and Everyone stay clean. The full legacy "Starts with / Then / Ends with ... from $N" wording (`Shelby.Data/EntityClassExtensions/POOrder.cs:120-166`; a one-level path reads "Ends with") as the column's accessible name and its hover. And **a dimmed column reading "Not required under $5,000"** when the level's minimum is above every amount on the board, which demonstrates the skip rule in behaviour rather than describing it in a caption.

**A drop is still an act on the live path.** Forward is any later level, or Approved; it records my approval at my level through the same cascade and the same stored rows as the record screen. Because approving at a level implies the ones below it (`POOrderRepository.cs:1754`), the model and not the drop decides where the card lands, and the confirm dialog names that column (`purFLandCol`). A leftward drop is refused, "Approval moves forward only". Rejected and hold refuse as before. Close is offered from every column, on hold or not (27 Sep ruling); Void applies to any open order, since nothing is paid, so the zone's caption drops "unpaid only".

**The rest of the widget follows.** The record pop-up loses the Payment Approval tab and the Payment Approval Path dropdown - exactly the two things `Requests/Update.aspx:93` hides for this flag - and nothing else about it changes, so the one-to-one ruling holds. The status chip is not offered on the path board, because the columns are the status axis (as Encumbrances already hides it); a stored lane falls back through the existing guard. The table status cell reads Pending approval / Approved. The legend is a separate function so the payment legend is untouched, and its Columns section is built from `purFCols`, so it cannot drift from the board. `PURF_ABOUT_NOPAY` describes the board it draws.

**Glance's third tile becomes "Waiting on others"** (requests where the open level is someone else's and the viewer is not on a later level). "To be paid" has no meaning with the process off, and the new tile completes the set the other way: mine now, mine later, someone else's. This is the one substitution that amends an existing ruling (27 Sep, Glance); **flagged for the owner at review.**

**Encumbrances is deliberately unchanged.** The legacy rule is open detail dollars while `Status < 2` and not rejected (`GLAccountRepository.cs:2220`) and never mentions the payment process; paid-ness is still real with the flag off, because AP enters and pays the invoice by hand. A driver assertion pins the encumbrance total as identical with the flag on and off, so the owner's signed-off figures do not move.
